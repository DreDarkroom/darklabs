CRACKSHOT

real recordings · sliced, measured, shaped · no generated audio

real recordingsno AI audioCC0 / CC BY

I was looking for a really good pistol shot for a game I'm working on, and ended up building a small workshop around it. It hunts through real, openly licensed gunshot recordings, slices them into single shots, measures each one, scores how well it suits a snappy, punchy game pistol, and then shapes the best ones to be crisp and crunchy. Everything is classic signal processing. Nothing here is generated by an AI audio model.

Turn your volume down a little: these are gunshots, and some of them are sharp.

How it's judged

The score is a plain checklist, not a mystery. You can read it, disagree with it and change it.

What "crisp" and "crunchy" mean here

Crisp is a clear, bright, quick transient: it speaks instantly, has a strong 2 to 6 kHz "crack", and doesn't smear. Crunchy is texture: dense, gritty harmonics in the 1 to 8 kHz range, the thing saturation adds, so the shot has teeth and not just a click. The numbers on this page are proxies for what you hear: crisp comes from brightness, attack and tail; crunch comes from how squashed the peak is (crest factor, kurtosis) and how dense the spectrum is.

The suitability score (0 to 100) and its checklist
  • Attack (14): how fast it reaches full volume. Under 1.6 ms is ideal.
  • Tail (11): time to fall 50 dB. 140 to 480 ms is right for a game pistol.
  • Dry (14): how little room echo is in the first half second. The game adds its own space.
  • Clipping (13): any shot where the recorder distorted is penalised hard.
  • Clean (7): the noise floor under the shot.
  • Crack (14): the share of energy in the 2 to 6 kHz band.
  • Body (10) and Weight (8): how much low end there is, so it feels like a gun and not a clap.
  • Bright (9): spectral centre between about 1.5 and 4.8 kHz.
How the shots are shaped
  • Tail control: an extra fade after 70 to 80 ms dries out the room.
  • Transient shaping: where a fast envelope runs ahead of a slow one, a little gain (up to about +6 dB) sharpens the first few milliseconds.
  • Crisp: a harmonic exciter on the 3.5 kHz+ band plus presence EQ.
  • Crunch: parallel saturation on 250 Hz to 7 kHz plus a touch of sample-and-hold grit, with the top end kept bright.
  • Weight: a short, plain sine "thump" falling from about 130 to 58 Hz, generated by code, mixed low so it supports the shot without slowing the attack.
  • Arc: the crisp version with a pitched-down, high-passed whisper of a sci-fi sound from Kenney's CC0 pack (the same pack the Sonic Smithy uses).
Match a sound you like (private, runs on your machine)

If there's a pistol sound you want to get close to, record a short clip of it for your own reference and run python tools/match_reference.py yourclip.wav. It measures your clip the same way and lists which shots here are nearest. Nothing is uploaded and your clip is never copied into the pack. My target profile (a punchy, bright, dry, short pistol) is an approximation; a real reference replaces my guess with your ear.
