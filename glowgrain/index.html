    How to play (start here)
    1. Think pads, not piano. Press Pads: eight pads, and every one sounds good because they only play notes from the current chord. Tap rhythms on them exactly as you would on a kit. Low pads are your kick-and-floor-tom (root, third, fifth); high pads are the cymbals-and-toms of the melody.
    2. Turn on Guide. On the keys, the glowing ones are the notes that fit the chord right now. Other notes are dimmed, and anything outside the key is nudged to the nearest note that is in it, so you can't hit a wrong one. The four dots by Bloom are the beat: 1 is the big one.
    3. Slide Bloom up slowly. A band builds underneath you: pad, bass, marimba, percussion, voice. You play over the top. Stay on the glowing notes and it will always sound intentional.
    4. Play the rhythm you already know. Steady quarter notes on one note is a great first part. Beat snap (in Tone & harmony; Guide turns it on) nudges a hit that's a hair early onto the beat, and tightens everything you record in a loop onto the grid, like a quantised pad. Your groove, made tidy.
    5. Loop it. Press Loop, wait out the count-in, play 4 bars, and it repeats. Press again to layer a second part on top, like overdubbing a groove, then a melody.
    6. Sustain (or hold Space) keeps piano and marimba ringing; it lets go by itself at each new chord so it never gets muddy.
    Tone & harmony

    Play with touch, mouse, your keyboard (A–', Z/X octave, Space sustain) or a MIDI keyboard. Slide across keys for glissando; press lower on a key to play louder.

    A felt piano that blooms. Headphones or good speakers recommended.
